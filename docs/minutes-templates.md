# Minutes templates

Admins can decide how exported meeting minutes look, so they match your house style.

## Where

**Settings ▾ → Minutes templates.** Everyone can open a template's preview; only administrators can make or change one.

## What a template sets

- **Name**, for example "Board minutes".
- **Title line.** `{name}` becomes the meeting's name and `{date}` the day it was recorded, for example `Minutes of {name}, {date}`.
- **Font**: Arial, Calibri, Times New Roman, Georgia or Verdana.
- **Colour** of the title and headings.
- **Logo**: a PNG or JPEG picture up to 1 MB, at the top left or top right of the first page. Save the template first, then add the logo.
- **Lay the minutes out on the organisation's letterhead**: uses the same Word and PDF letterheads as dictated letters (Letterhead page). The reference line then moves to the end of the minutes, so it does not clash with the letterhead's own footer.
- **Parts**: Summary, Action points, Minutes, Tone of the meeting and Transcript. Give each a heading, put them in order with the arrows, and tick the ones included by default.
- **Show the time beside each line of the transcript**, or not.
- **Footer text**, for example "Confidential". The meeting's name, the Draft or Approved marker and the reference are always added after it.
- **Use this template by default.**

The title, the Draft or Approved marker and the details table are always included.

## Exporting with a template

On a meeting, **Export ▾** shows a **Template** list once a template exists. The default template is chosen. Choosing another one sets the ticks to that template's parts; you can still change them. "VerbaFlow standard" is VerbaFlow's own layout.

The History of the meeting records which template each export used.

## Checking

**Preview (PDF)** and **Download a sample (Word)** show a made-up meeting laid out with the template as last saved. Samples are not recorded as exports.

## Good to know

- A Word file names the font; the person opening it needs that font on their computer (all five are standard on Windows).
- Dictations (letters, Word documents, emails) do not use minutes templates; they use the letter layout.
- Not yet checked in Microsoft Word itself.
